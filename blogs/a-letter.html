A letter to my parents and to my friends who I haven’t seen in a few months

November 22nd, 2026

Look guys, I made the news!

Thanksgiving is coming soon and maybe the singularity some time after that. In August, Eli’s median for TED-AI was mid-2032. It is now September 2028. That’s about 1,385 days removed from his previous forecast made 86 days ago. Another way of understanding this is 16 days of expected ordinary time lost per day lived. Romeo’s median moved from early 2035, a loss of about 6.4 years. They say the world will be drastically different by the end of 2027. If these forecasters are right, we have about 57 Saturdays left of the world we grew up in. There will be about 14 full moons. I will spend approximately 20 full days with my parents.

There are a few things the calendar will give us only once or twice more before then.

  • Thanksgiving is four days away. After it, there is one more before the end of 2027.
  • One or two birthdays for each person you love, including you. I turn 20 in December, and the world looks even stranger than it did last year.
  • One or two springs, and one or two summers.
  • One leap day, February 29, 2028.
  • Maybe one Olympics, in Los Angeles in July 2028.

There are some things that are harder to count: how many meals I will share with someone, how many walks I will go on, how many times I will get to listen to the muted chatter of the people I love from the next room. It’s possible I will have an abundance of these experiences in the future, if things go well. I can imagine some things that are easier to lose: I do not know how many times again I will call that one friend because they are the only person who understands what’s going through my head; I do not know how many hours I get to invest into working on hard questions, or how many times I will feel again the thrill of coming to some truly novel revelation from my own contemplations.

Working in Berkeley, I’m close enough to feel how fast things are moving. Some days this work feels like a current pulling me further and further from the shores of normalcy. Most people can’t feel it yet, but the same water is carrying everyone else out too. I spoke to a friend recently who said he stopped telling his loved ones about what might happen (there isn’t much they can do and it’s better to spare them the fear of it). I understand his sentiment, and I’m not entirely sure why I’m writing this. Maybe I just want to say I love you. I don’t know yet if I’ll go back to school. I miss that place and my friends there, who I haven’t seen in months.

I’d like many of the numbers I mentioned in this post to be bigger. I have little control over many of them, but not all. I treat myself often. I linger in the doorway when I leave my friends. I wake up before the sun to get work done, so I can put every hour I have toward making a difference. The game might be over soon, and I’m trying to leave it all on the field.

If you’ve been meaning to call, call. I’ll pick up.

Much love,
Evie
